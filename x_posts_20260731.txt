==================================================
1. BEIGE BOOK HOLD
==================================================
The Fed's Beige Book just put the US AI story in one flat line.

Employers held head counts steady and invested further in AI.

Not mass layoffs. Capital first. Headcount later.

If you work in the US, the risk right now is quieter than a pink slip. It is your tasks getting reassigned while the title stays the same.

Which tasks on your desk already feel "AI optional"?

3. ENTRY LEVEL THINNING
==================================================
US companies are not announcing AI layoffs at scale.

They are quietly skipping the junior hire.

Entry tasks that used to train new grads (drafting, cleanup, first-pass review) are exactly what models do well. So the job stays. The first rung disappears.

If you are early career in the US right now, stop competing on polish. Compete on judgment, taste, and ownership.

What was your first real job's "grunt work" that AI would finish in ten minutes today?

==================================================
5. PREPARE ACT
==================================================
Congress is finally admitting the US does not have clean data on how AI is changing jobs.

The AI Workforce PREPARE Act wants Labor to track which tasks get automated, which skills rise, and when AI shows up in mass layoffs.

Boring bill. Important signal.

The US debate is shifting from sci-fi unemployment to: measure the shift, retrain for it, stop guessing.

Would you trust federal AI job data if it existed tomorrow?

7. ADOPTERS STILL HIRE
==================================================
Revelio's US tracker keeps repeating a pattern most headlines skip.

Firms that actually adopt AI are still growing headcount faster than firms that do not. Senior roles grow hardest. Junior roles grow slower.

So the US split is not "AI company vs human company."

It is "company that knows how to aim AI" vs everyone else.

Which side is your employer on, honestly?

==================================================
9. SCREENING ARMS RACE
==================================================
US hiring is stuck in a loop.

Candidates use AI to polish applications. Managers use AI to screen them. Both sides say the other made the pile worse.

Greenhouse says most seekers have already talked to an AI interviewer. Managers say AI resumes slowed everything down.

The edge in US hiring right now is not more AI. It is better signals: work samples, live problem solving, references that still mean something.

What signal would you trust in the next hire you make?

10. JUDGMENT EDGE
==================================================
IMAGES: us-images/20260706/us-img-05.png | linkedin-infographic-20260701.png
CAPTION:
US firms can buy the same models. They cannot buy the same judgment.

Apollo, the Fed, and the labor trackers all rhyme: output is getting cheaper. Direction is not.

If your American job is "make the thing," pressure rises.
If your job is "decide what is worth making," leverage rises.

Write down one decision you make weekly that no tool should own. Protect that skill.

==================================================
11. PRODUCTIVITY NOT DOOM
==================================================
US productivity gains from AI are still measured in fractions of a percent in some surveys. Not the apocalypse slide deck.

That is why the pain feels weird. Slow. Uneven. Concentrated in junior and high-exposure roles.

Mass unemployment is a bad map for 2026 America.
Task thinning plus wage pressure is a better one.

Are you preparing for the loud scenario or the quiet one?
